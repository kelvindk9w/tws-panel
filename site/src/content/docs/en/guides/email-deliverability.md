This guide shows how to bring your domain's email **in line with the Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft sender
requirements**. That raises the chances of a message arriving quickly and landing in the inbox. **No
server can guarantee inbox placement**: the final decision belongs to the recipient's provider and also
depends on your IP reputation and on the content.

Examples use `example.com` and `203.0.113.10`. Replace them with your own values.

## Why the first email can take a few minutes

With a new IP and domain, Gmail often answers "try again later" (codes `421 4.7.28` or `4.7.0`). The
server keeps the message and retries; the first retry is about 2 minutes later. This fades after weeks of
regular sending, at a low and growing volume, without complaints. The panel shows the cause under
**Email sending health**, in the "Sending queue" item.

Note: if the reason is `4.7.26`, `4.7.27`, `4.7.30` or `4.7.32`, **it is not a rate limit**. It is an
authentication failure (SPF, DKIM or sender domain alignment) and waiting does not fix it: check SPF and
DKIM below and the domain used as the sender.

> [!NOTE]
> **Registering a domain restarts the mail server** for a few seconds, because Stalwart only signs with the
> new DKIM key after a restart. Open connections drop and come back; the sending queue is kept. If the
> restart fails, the panel shows DKIM as **attention (restart pending)**, and messages go out unsigned
> until the next sync.

## 1. Set up DNS in order

Open the domain on the Email page. The **Email sending health** card has the **DNS step by step**, with
the records in order and copy buttons:

1. **A** for `mail.example.com` pointing to the server IP.
2. **MX** for the domain pointing to `mail.example.com`.
3. **SPF** (TXT) authorizing the server IP.
4. **DKIM** (TXT at `paas._domainkey`), the public key generated by the panel.
5. **DMARC** (TXT at `_dmarc`), starting at `p=none` and tightening later.
6. Optional: **TLS-RPT** and **MTA-STS**.

### Cloudflare: gray cloud

In the Cloudflare dashboard, under **DNS**, the `A` records for `mail` (and `autoconfig`, if you have
it) must be **gray cloud** ("DNS only"). With the orange cloud (proxy), other servers see Cloudflare's IP,
which does not receive email, and MX and SPF stop matching. `MX` and `TXT` records are never proxied. In
the MX field the mail server is `mail.example.com` and the priority is `10`.

### PTR (reverse DNS) at the VPS provider

The PTR is **not** in your domain's DNS. You set it in the VPS provider's dashboard (a "reverse DNS" or
"rDNS" field on the IP screen). It must point to the server name shown in the panel, for example
`mail.example.com`, and that name needs an A record for the same IP. Without a PTR, many providers delay
or reject the message.

The PTR is **per server IP, not per project or domain**: a single PTR, pointing to the mail server name
(the same one used in HELO/EHLO, which must have an A record back to the IP), covers every project. On
**Contabo**: Customer Control Panel, open the server, go to IPs/Network, click Reverse DNS next to the IP,
type the name and save. On other hosts look for "Reverse DNS", "rDNS" or "PTR"; if there is none, open a
ticket. The panel shows the expected name, the value found and ready-made text ("Please set the reverse
DNS (PTR) of IP X to the name Y") filled in with your own VPS data.

Why this is **important attention** and not critical: with SPF, DKIM and DMARC passing, Gmail accepted
messages without a PTR. Microsoft, Yahoo and corporate servers may delay or refuse, so fix it before
sending in volume.

## 2. Read the health panel

Click **Check health now** (limit of 6 per minute). Each item has a state:

- **OK**: in line.
- **Attention**: works, but may hurt delivery (1024-bit DKIM key, inherited DMARC, certificate close to expiry, DNS still propagating).
- **Critical**: likely to block or badly hurt sending (blocked port 25, SPF, DKIM or DMARC failure, stopped server). A red notice lists these items. Attention items that also affect delivery (like the PTR) appear in an orange notice.

Items come in two groups: **Server** (IP, reverse DNS, port 25, certificate, blocklists and queue, which apply to every project) and **Domain** (A, MX, SPF, DKIM, DMARC, propagation, MTA-STS and TLS-RPT, per domain). On the page of each project with email, the Sender tab shows the project domain's summary and the state of the PTR and port 25.

Every item that is not in order has a **What to do** button with short steps and values to copy. Bonus items (MTA-STS, TLS-RPT) say they are optional and what they improve. When DMARC is `p=none` and all is well, the panel suggests, without forcing, moving to `p=quarantine` only after 2 to 4 weeks of good reports; the risk is a legitimate message that fails going to spam, and you can go back to `p=none`.

Items: A, MX, SPF (including the 10 DNS lookups limit), DKIM, DMARC, PTR, outbound port 25, queue, TLS
certificate, blocklists, propagation and, as a bonus, MTA-STS and TLS-RPT. The panel repeats the check
every day. IP reputation and complaints cannot be measured from here: use Google Postmaster Tools and
Microsoft SNDS.

Changed DNS? Wait: it can take minutes to hours. The "Propagation" item warns when resolvers still
disagree.

## 3. Test and check in Gmail

Under **Send test email**, type an address of yours and send (at most 60 per day). After "delivered":

1. Open the message in Gmail.
2. Click the three dots and **Show original**.
3. Check that **SPF**, **DKIM** and **DMARC** show **PASS**.
4. See whether it landed in Spam; if so, mark it "Not spam" to help.

The panel does not read your mailbox; it only reports the server-side send result.
